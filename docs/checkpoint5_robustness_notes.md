# Checkpoint 5, step 1: robustness on the full 2022 train split

Numbers: `docs/checkpoint5_robustness_report.md` (generated by `scripts/compare_robustness.py`).
Four variants × seeds 42/43/44, each trained on all 6,509 train diaries for 40 epochs (best validation
checkpoint). All evaluation on the 1,627 validation diaries; probes fitted on train-split embeddings.
2023 untouched. Mean ± SD over seeds.

## Caveat: training budget

11 of 12 runs had their best checkpoint at the last epoch, and validation reconstruction was still falling
(about 0.02 nats per 5 epochs). All variants share the same budget, so comparisons are fair, but absolute
reconstruction numbers are conservative.

## Findings

**Full data helps every variant.** Accuracy is 0.81-0.83 (0.73-0.76 on the 400-diary subset) and macro-F1
0.59-0.64. Predicted transitions per day rose to 5.6-6.4, still far below the true 14.8, so oversmoothing
remains the main reconstruction weakness.

**Learned latents clear the untrained floor on non-linear probes.** With kNN probes, the untrained
encoder adds essentially nothing about timing beyond totals (0.003). Every trained timing latent adds
0.21-0.23. Linear probes could not show this, because their untrained floor is 0.19, so kNN probes are
the informative ones.

**Most of the separation comes from the amount-prediction head, not from invariance.** Compared with
`unguided`, the `no_invariance` model (amount prediction only):
- cuts amount pair sensitivity from ~0.26 to 0.04-0.06;
- raises kNN totals R² from the amount latent from 0.35 to 0.83;
- reduces timing leakage into the amount latent (kNN gain 0.12 → 0.02);
- makes decoded timing follow the timing source on matched-totals pairs (0.64 → 0.73).

**Invariance (λ=1) adds a cleaner amount latent at a real reconstruction cost.** Against `no_invariance`:
- amount pair sensitivity 0.011/0.017 vs 0.044/0.061;
- kNN totals R² 0.849 vs 0.825;
- timing leakage into amount 0.008 vs 0.019;
- but validation CE is 0.580 vs 0.521 (+11%) and macro-F1 0.588 vs 0.623;
- and timing-latent measures are no better (kNN timing gain 0.220 vs 0.231; matched mix-and-match
  0.714 vs 0.732).

**No variant carries more timing information than the joint model.** The single 24-dim latent's kNN
timing gain (0.230) equals the split models' timing latents. The split adds an interpretable division of
labour, with an amount part that predicts totals and resists rearrangements, not extra timing information.

## Consequence for the pre-registered λ rule

λ = 1 was chosen on the 400-diary subset with the rule "validation CE within 5% of no_invariance". At full
data, λ = 1 is 11% worse, so it **no longer satisfies that rule**. Before freezing the pipeline, either
re-run the λ sweep on full data (e.g. λ ∈ {0.1, 0.3}) and re-apply the same rule, or freeze
`no_invariance` (amount prediction only) as the main model and keep λ = 1 as a reported ablation.
This is a validation-only decision.

## Full-data λ sweep (added after the runs above)

λ ∈ {0.1, 0.3} × 3 seeds, same 40-epoch budget. Validation CE ratio to no_invariance: λ=1 1.113,
λ=0.3 1.037, λ=0.1 1.039. Mean amount pair sensitivity: 0.014 / 0.024 / 0.033. By the unchanged rule,
**λ = 0.3** is frozen for the final models (`configs/vae_final.yaml`).

## Cluster stability (validation, 40-epoch runs)

k=6 k-means on timing latents across seeds 42/43/44 gives low agreement. With standardized latents,
cross-seed ARI was 0.02-0.12. Unstandardized (now the frozen choice, because the prior already sets latent
scale) it was guided 0.25, no_invariance 0.12, unguided 0.17, joint 0.35. Within-seed bootstrap ARI is
0.47-0.65, against 0.70 for engineered timing features in Checkpoint 2. The learned latents do not have
stable k-means structure at k=6.
