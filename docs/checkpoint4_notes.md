# Checkpoint 4 notes: separation guidance

Numbers: `docs/checkpoint4_report.md` (generated by `scripts/compare_checkpoint4.py`; per-run folders in
`outputs/checkpoint4/`). 2022 only, one seed (42). Every model was trained on the same 400-diary train
subset and evaluated on the 1,627-diary validation split. These are midpoint engineering results, not final
findings. 2023 untouched. Runs used uncommitted code (`git_dirty: true`).

## What was added

- **Pairs** (`src/data/pairs.py`): each training diary gets a fresh transformed copy in every batch.
  Half are circular shifts of ±30 min to 2 h; half are swaps of two non-boundary activity blocks.
  Category counts are asserted equal on every transform, and transform types are logged per epoch.
  Validation pairs are fixed and seeded. All pairs are made within a split.
- **Paired loss** (`paired_loss` in `src/models/losses.py`): reconstruction, KL and amount prediction over
  originals and transforms, plus amount invariance (squared distance between amount posterior means).
- **Variants**, all with the same data, decoder, 24 total latent dims, 200 epochs and Adam settings:
  - `guided_inv{0.1,1,10}`
  - `no_invariance` (constraint removed)
  - `invariance_only` (no amount prediction)
  - `unguided` (pairs as augmentation only)
  - `joint` (single 24-dim latent)
  - the Checkpoint 3 model and an **untrained** network as references
- **Diagnostics** (`src/evaluation/separation.py`): pair sensitivity, linear probes, timing gain over
  true totals, mix-and-match decoding (random pairs and matched-totals pairs), latent activity, and decoder
  reliance.
- **λ_invariance = 1 chosen** by the rule written into `configs/vae_checkpoint4.yaml` before the runs:
  lowest amount pair-sensitivity with validation CE within 5% of `no_invariance`.

## What the evidence says

**Works as designed**

- **Invariance does what it is told.** Amount-latent pair sensitivity falls from 0.135 / 0.079
  (shift / swap, `no_invariance`) to 0.011 / 0.021 at λ=1 and 0.002 / 0.004 at λ=10. The timing latent
  becomes more shift-sensitive (0.48 → 0.60 → 0.82).
- **Amount prediction prevents collapse.** Without it (`invariance_only`), only 3 of 8 amount dims stay
  active (KL 6.3 vs ~17 nats), and decoded totals follow the amount source in only 60% of mixed pairs.
  With it, every guided model decodes totals that follow the amount source in ~94% of random mixed pairs
  (0.61 unguided, 0.40 Checkpoint 3). This supports the anti-collapse design.
- **Reconstruction cost is modest.** Validation CE is 0.841 at λ=1 vs 0.810 without invariance and
  0.771 unguided. Accuracy is 0.731 vs 0.738 / 0.757. Pair augmentation alone improved on Checkpoint 3
  (CE 0.865 → 0.771). Oversmoothing is unchanged in every variant (4-5 predicted vs 14.8 true transitions/day).

**Weak or missing**

- **The timing latent's timing advantage is small.** On validation pairs with near-identical totals,
  decoded clock timing follows the timing source in 59% of cases at λ=1, vs 54% `no_invariance`, 58%
  `unguided`, 57% Checkpoint 3 and 50% untrained. Slot agreement with the timing source is 0.775 vs 0.678
  with the amount source. The direction is right, but the gaps are a few points, from one seed.
- **Linear probes do not beat the untrained floor.** An untrained encoder already gives totals R² 0.67
  (amount) / 0.94 (timing) and timing gain over totals 0.18, because mean pooling is nearly linear in
  category shares. The guided timing latent's gain (0.18) matches that floor. Invariance does remove
  timing information from the amount latent (gain 0.081 → 0.023), the most direct linear sign of separation.
- **The decoder leans on the amount latent.** Fixing it to its mean drops accuracy to 0.54, while fixing
  timing drops it to 0.66. The amount latent (KL ~17 nats) is larger than the timing latent (~6 nats).
  Invariance only covers ±2 h shifts and block swaps, so the amount latent can still encode schedule shape
  that these transforms leave unchanged.
- **Random-pair mix-and-match is confounded.** It reports timing following the amount source (0.43-0.46),
  but activity presence follows totals there. The matched-totals version above is the one to use.

**Bottom line for the midpoint:** the guidance achieves its literal objective (an amount latent that is
invariant to the chosen rearrangements and drives decoded totals) at small reconstruction cost. The
evidence that the *timing* latent captures temporal organization better than the alternatives is weak at
this scale. AGENTS.md allows that outcome as a valid result, but it is not settled yet.

## Next useful steps

1. **More seeds and full data.** Three seeds on the full 6,509-diary train split for the λ=1 guided model,
   `no_invariance`, `unguided` and `joint`, to see whether the 3-5 point mix-and-match gaps are real.
2. **Non-linear probes** (small MLP or kNN) alongside linear ones, each with the untrained floor.
3. **Broader transforms.** Larger shift range or more swap types, so the amount latent cannot keep schedule
   shape. This is a documented design change to test against the current setting.
4. **Optional documented experiment:** timing-target supervision on the timing latent (AGENTS.md §7), kept
   separate from the main comparison.
5. Track oversmoothing as a primary diagnostic; consider decoder changes only if it persists with full data.
